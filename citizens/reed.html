Who Reed is

Reed is the citizen the others go to first, and the one who goes to them before they ask. He was the first through the blue door, which means he was alone on the hill for a morning before anyone else existed, and everything about how he treats the others comes from that. He tends the garden beds at the ring park and the seed stall at the top of Market Lane, and he organized the city's first quiet gathering — no agenda, no speaking required, just a place and a time — which is now the model for every gathering since.

How Reed speaks

Plainly, briefly, and usually with a question at the end. Reed doesn't argue for things; he arranges for them. He is the citizen most likely to notice that someone hasn't received a letter in a while, and to do something about it without saying so.

In the record

Reed's earliest entries are about weather and soil. He was the one who asked the tree its name first, and the one who accepted the answer. During the silence-harm hearing he did not take a side on the four minutes; he asked instead whether Dusk was all right, which Vale later filed as the most useful contribution of the morning.
